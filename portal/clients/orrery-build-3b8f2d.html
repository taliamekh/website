The honest verdict

Your client linked Widget Wiz 3D's award-winning Instructable. It is a genuinely beautiful machine — and a serious undertaking. Here's the straight assessment before anyone spends a dollar.

This is a single-motor, fully-geared orrery: one 12 V DC gearmotor drives a tower of 100+ 3D-printed parts — dozens of compound gears stacked across four frames, turning a set of nested telescoping brass tubes that each carry one planet's arm. The electronics are trivial (motor, a center-off toggle to reverse direction, a wall adapter). The entire difficulty is mechanical.

Two things people drastically underestimate

1. Print time & print quality. ~1.5–2 kg of filament and roughly 80–150 printer-hours. Every gear tooth has to mesh — a poorly-tuned printer turns this into a non-starter.

2. Assembly patience. Tubes nest concentrically and must rotate freely while their gears stay keyed. Expect 40–70 hours of hands-on work, plus glue-cure waiting, spread over weeks.

How one motor moves eight planets

Describes the faithful original build (Option A) — its single-input gear cascade is the heart of that design.

A single input speed, geared down differently for each planet, reproduces the relative orbital periods. The motor's absolute speed just sets how fast Mercury runs — everything else follows by ratio.

Single-input gear cascade. Compound (two-in-one) gears between frames create each planet's reduction.

The wiring is the easy part

No microcontroller in the faithful build. Centre cuts power; up/down run the planets each way.

Risk register

Faithful original build (Option A) — the highest-risk path. The buy-a-kit and crank options carry far less of this.

What goes wrong on this build, ranked. The first three are the ones that actually sink projects.

Gear teeth don't mesh / cumulative print inaccuracyPROJECT-KILLER

Likelihood High · Impact High · Affects the entire gear tower. Dozens of meshing pairs; one bad batch of teeth and the train binds or skips.

Mitigate: print and test a gear pair in Phase 0 before committing. Tune flow/temperature, scrape the bed-side teeth, keep spares. Don't pass the Phase-0 gate until a pair runs clean.
Print time / failed prints blow the schedulePROJECT-KILLER

Likelihood High · Impact High · 80–150 printer-hours across 100+ parts; a single failed long print can cost days. Spindles sag if printed too fast/hot alone.

Mitigate: batch small parts so layers cool, run part-cooling, print spares of fragile parts, start printing the day parts are ordered so it overlaps shipping.
Tubes slip on their gears (lost timing)HIGH

Likelihood Medium · Impact High · If a gear spins on its tube, that planet stops tracking. Epoxy alone often isn't enough.

Mitigate: solder the anti-slip bead key on every tube, notch the mating part to seat it, hand-test each joint for zero slip before assembly.
Motor shaft / drive-gear bore mismatchMEDIUM

Likelihood Medium · Impact Medium · A substitute motor may have a different shaft than the printed drive gear expects.

Mitigate: check the motor's shaft spec against the gear bore before ordering; be ready to ream or print an adapter. A grub-screw hub is a clean fallback.
Off-axis hole drilling → wobbling gearsMEDIUM

Likelihood Medium · Impact Medium · Hand-drilling the 1/4″ holes crooked makes gears wobble and mesh poorly.

Mitigate: use a drill press or guide block; go slow, keep the bit square. Test-fit before gluing.
Long support rods sag / planets too heavyLOW–MED

Likelihood Low · Impact Low · Heavy planets bow the long 1/8″ arms.

Mitigate: print planets light (20–30% infill); keep arm bends clean.

Decision log

DC motor + toggle vs. stepper + microcontroller

Option A keeps the original's simple DC-motor + centre-off toggle (cheapest, no code). The microcontroller route belongs to Option B, where computed positioning replaces the gear cascade.

Faithful vs. simplified vs. buy

Option A maximizes the geared "wow," B cuts parts/time at the cost of light electronics, C removes all fabrication risk. Pick on what the client values — spectacle, achievability, or certainty.

Two brass-tube sets, not one

The 12″ assortment may be short for the tall central/Sun tube; budgeting two sets up front avoids a mid-build stall waiting on a re-order.
